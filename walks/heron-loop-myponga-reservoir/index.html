The Walk

The Heron Loop is the easy short circuit at Myponga Reservoir Reserve — a relaxed 1.9 km that takes in two of the reserve’s best reservoir viewpoints. Named for the herons that work the reservoir margins in the quieter morning hours, it’s a step up from the flat Spinebill but still accessible to any level of fitness. Allow half an hour at a comfortable pace, more if you stop at the lookouts.

What You’ll See

From the Hansen St car park the trail winds through reserve bushland — formed gravel, gentle grades, no challenging terrain — to the Deck Lookout. This is a timber viewing platform built at the edge of the reserve with a direct outlook over the Myponga Reservoir. On still mornings the water mirrors the surrounding hills; in the afternoon it picks up wind riffles. Either way it’s a genuinely attractive view, and this is the best point in the reserve to look for herons and other water birds in the margins below the deck.

The trail continues past the deck to the Reservoir Lookout, a more open viewpoint that takes in a wider sweep of the water and the valley. From here the circuit passes through the Eatts St car park area — where the reserve’s public toilets are located — before returning along the southern edge of the reserve to the Hansen St starting point.

Birdwatching Notes

The Heron name isn’t accidental. The SA Water reserve environment — low foot traffic, undisturbed water, native vegetation on the margins — makes this a better birdwatching destination than more popular trail areas. Beyond herons, you can expect ducks, cormorants and various small bush birds in the surrounding vegetation. Morning visits produce the most activity; the reserve is at its quietest in the hour after gates open.

Practical Notes

Toilets are at the Eatts St car park — roughly at the midpoint of the loop — not at the Hansen St start. Carry water, as there’s nothing on the trail. Dogs are not permitted anywhere in the reserve.

For access, opening hours, facilities and the full rules that apply to every Myponga loop, see the Myponga Reservoir Walks guide.

Highlights

  • Two reservoir viewpoints in under 2 km of easy, well-graded walking
  • Quiet, low-traffic reserve setting ideal for birdwatching
  • Connects directly to the Kestrel Loop from the same Hansen St trailhead

Practical Information

Tips for Walkers

The Heron Loop is an easy walk — but a few details make the difference between a good visit and a frustrating one.

Check Gate Times Before Visiting

Myponga Reservoir Reserve is gated — opening hours change with the season and gates are locked outside those hours. Check current access times with SA Water before heading out. The Heron Loop takes about 30 minutes, so the timing risk is low, but arriving to find the reserve locked is still a wasted trip.

Morning Visits for the Best Birdwatching

The Heron Loop takes its name from the herons that frequent the reservoir margins. Morning visits — particularly between April and October — produce the best birdwatching at both the Deck Lookout and the Reservoir Lookout. The SA Water reserve is lightly visited, which is part of what makes it good for birds: low foot traffic, undisturbed water margins and plenty of native cover.

Extending the Day

The Heron Loop shares the Hansen St trailhead with the Kestrel Loop — the full 6.8 km reserve circuit. If 1.9 km leaves you wanting more, continue onto the Kestrel from the same starting point. The Lapwing Loop (3.9 km) is accessible from the Eatts St car park, which the Heron Loop passes through at roughly the midpoint.

Two Trailheads — Start from Hansen St

The Heron Loop starts from the Hansen St car park but passes through the Eatts St car park partway around (where the toilets are). Park at Hansen St for the best experience — the circuit is designed to be walked in the order described. Arriving at Eatts St instead gives you direct access to the Spinebill and Lapwing loops rather than the Heron.
