The Walk

The Boundary Loop is the full perimeter circuit of Happy Valley Reservoir. At 10.16 km with 173 m of total ascent, it is the most demanding walk in the reserve and the only one that circumnavigates the entire water body. Walking SA rates it Grade 4 Challenging, citing very steep hills throughout — and unlike the Grade 4 Shoreline Loop which is undulating, the Boundary Loop’s climbs are sharper and more concentrated.

This is the walk for people who’ve already done the Shoreline Loop and want to see the rest of the reservoir. It takes a substantially different route, following the outer boundary of the reserve rather than tracing the water’s edge. This means more climbing, more exposure on the western ridge, and — on a clear day — better views across to the outer suburbs and hills.

The trail is also open to mountain bikes and is regularly used by trail runners, making it the most active-use circuit in the network. Weekday mornings are quieter; weekend mornings can be busy near the carpark end.

The Terrain

The elevation range of 148 m to 201 m sounds modest, but the route achieves it via multiple sharp ascents rather than a gradual rise. The western boundary section involves sustained steep climbing and is the point where most walkers will feel the grade most acutely. Good trail shoes with grip are appropriate; poles are useful for the steeper descents.

The surface is consistent with the rest of the Happy Valley network — formed gravel and compacted dirt throughout. There are no technical sections, but the steep grade on loose gravel demands sure footing.

Glenthorne National Park

A pedestrian crossing at the South Road / Black Road intersection connects the Boundary Loop directly to Glenthorne National Park. Glenthorne is a developing national park on the southern fringe of the metropolitan area — trail infrastructure is being expanded progressively. The connection point means a longer day walk combining both reserves is feasible, though you’ll need to research current Glenthorne trail conditions separately as they are not covered here.

Access Points

The main trailhead at the Chandlers Hill Road / Kenihans Road roundabout is the standard start point. A secondary carpark near the dam wall provides alternative access from Manning Road, which is useful for accessing the Minkarra Park section or if the main carpark is full on busy mornings.

Practical Notes

  • Allow 3 hours at a steady walking pace — more if you add Glenthorne
  • Very steep hills on the western boundary — trekking poles recommended
  • Carry 1.5–2 litres of water; no fountains on course beyond the trailhead
  • Mountain bikes and trail runners share the trail — stay aware
  • Reserve closes on Total Fire Ban days — check cfs.sa.gov.au
  • Gates locked at closing time — 5:00 pm in winter; don’t start after 2:00 pm
  • No dogs anywhere in the reserve
  • Green trail markers throughout — follow carefully at junctions near the trailhead

Practical Information

Tips for Walkers

The most demanding of the four Happy Valley tracks — very steep hills, full sun exposure on the western boundary, and 10 km of sustained terrain. Take this seriously.

Opening Hours

The reserve is open 7:30 am to 8:00 pm October to March, and 7:30 am to 5:00 pm April to September. At 3 hours for a fit walker, don't start this loop after 2:00 pm in winter.

Fire Ban Closures

The reserve closes completely on Total Fire Ban days. Check cfs.sa.gov.au before every visit. Given the exposed western boundary section, this loop is particularly unpleasant in extreme heat even when the reserve is open.

Very Steep Hills

Walking SA rates this Grade 4 Challenging and specifically notes very steep hills throughout. With 173 m of total ascent concentrated into repeated sharp climbs — particularly on the western boundary — this is a genuinely demanding circuit. Good fitness is required, not just average.

Green Trail Markers

The Boundary Loop uses green trail markers. Follow these carefully at junctions — the Boundary Loop shares sections of trail with the other loops near the trailhead before branching onto the outer perimeter.

Glenthorne National Park Connection

A pedestrian crossing at the South Road / Black Road intersection connects the Boundary Loop to Glenthorne National Park. This allows for extended walks combining the reservoir circuit with Glenthorne's trail network on the same outing.

Mountain Biking Permitted

The Boundary Loop is suitable for mountain biking, unlike the shorter Woodland and Southern loops. Trail runners also use this circuit regularly. Be aware of other users — give way to walkers and announce yourself when overtaking.

Secondary Carpark

A secondary carpark near the dam wall and Manning Road provides an alternative access point for Minkarra Park. This is useful if the main Chandlers Hill carpark is full on busy weekend mornings.

No Dogs

Dogs are strictly prohibited throughout the entire reserve. This applies on the Boundary Loop as on all other Happy Valley tracks.
