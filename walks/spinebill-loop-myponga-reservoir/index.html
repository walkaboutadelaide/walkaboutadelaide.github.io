The Walk

The Spinebill Loop is the starter at Myponga Reservoir Reserve — the reserve’s shortest bird-named trail and the natural choice if you’re stopping at Myponga on the way south to the coast, walking with small children or simply want a quick break with a proper water view.

At 700 metres on flat, well-maintained formed gravel trail, it’s a fifteen-minute round trip to the Reservoir Lookout and back. The climbing is almost nonexistent — about 18 metres of gentle undulation — and the surface is reliable underfoot year-round. That simplicity is the point.

What You’ll See

From the Eatts St car park the trail runs gently across the edge of the reserve to the Reservoir Lookout, an open viewpoint across the Myponga Reservoir water storage. The reservoir sits in a horseshoe of rolling hills — farmland and native bush on the higher slopes, the water still and reflective below. On calm mornings the views across the water are quietly beautiful.

The reserve attracts good birdlife precisely because it’s low-traffic. SA Water reserves tend to be undervisited relative to national park walking areas, and Myponga is no exception. Even on the short Spinebill trail it’s worth slowing down at the lookout and watching the margins of the reservoir for water birds.

Practical Notes

Toilets and parking are at the Eatts St car park — use the toilets before setting off, as there are no facilities out on the trail. No dogs are permitted anywhere in the Myponga Reservoir Reserve.

For everything that applies to all the Myponga loops — access, gated opening hours, reserve rules and facilities — see the Myponga Reservoir Walks guide rather than repeating it here.

Practical Information

Tips for Walkers

The Spinebill is genuinely simple — but a few things are worth knowing before you go.

Check Gate Times Before You Go

Myponga Reservoir Reserve has gated opening hours that change with the season. Gates are locked outside these hours — arriving to find the reserve closed is a frustrating experience. Check current opening times with SA Water before heading out, particularly on shorter winter days when gates close in the mid-afternoon.

Combine With a Longer Loop

The Spinebill shares the Eatts St car park with the Lapwing Loop (3.9 km) and feeds into the Kestrel Loop (6.8 km). If you arrive with more energy than the 700 m Spinebill requires, it's easy to extend — just continue past the Reservoir Lookout onto the Lapwing circuit from the same trailhead.

Best Time for Birdwatching

Despite the short distance, the Reservoir Lookout is genuinely good for birdlife. The SA Water reserve has low foot traffic, and the water attracts species you won't see as readily in typical parkland. Early mornings between April and October are the best time — bring binoculars if you have them.

Part of a Bird-Named Family of Loops

The Spinebill is the first of four bird-named loops at Myponga Reservoir Reserve — Spinebill, Heron, Lapwing and Kestrel, each progressively longer. All share the same trailheads and can be combined on a single visit. See the Myponga Reservoir Walks guide for the full picture.
