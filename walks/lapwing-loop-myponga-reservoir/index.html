The Walk

The Lapwing Loop is the mid-length option at Myponga Reservoir Reserve and, for most walkers, the most rewarding of the shorter circuits. At 3.9 km with 108 metres of climbing, it threads together the reserve’s most interesting terrain: the forest walk section through denser native bush, the old homestead ruins, and the reservoir outlook — and does it in just over an hour at a comfortable pace.

The character of the Lapwing is different from the flatter Spinebill and Heron loops. Where those circuits mostly follow the formed gravel trail at the edge of the reservoir, the Lapwing pushes into the bushland on the western margin of the reserve, where the vegetation is thicker and the trail has more of the feel of genuine bush walking.

Through the Forest

Starting from the Eatts St car park, the Lapwing climbs gently away from the reservoir through reserve bushland. The gradient is moderate rather than steep — short rises separated by flatter sections — and the surface transitions from the formed gravel path near the car park to the more natural surface of the forest walk section.

The Forest Walk Lookout is reached after the main climb, with elevated views back across the reservoir through the native vegetation. The forest walk section itself is the highlight of the Lapwing — denser, quieter, more characterful than the open sections near the water. This is where the birdwatching is best: honeyeaters working through the flowering understorey, grey fantails following you along the trail, thornbills in the low scrub.

The Homestead Ruins

The Homestead Ruin Lookout is a lookout near the site of farmstead ruins within the reserve — remnants of the farming properties that occupied the Myponga valley before the reservoir was built in the 1960s. SA Water acquired and cleared the land at that time; the ruins are what remains of one of those original homesteads. It’s worth taking a moment here — the outlook across the southern section of the reservoir is good, and the contrast between the managed reserve of today and the farming past gives the landscape an additional layer of interest.

Reservoir Outlook and Return

From the homestead ruins the trail descends gently back toward the water, passing the Reservoir Lookout for an open view across the reservoir before returning to the Eatts St car park. This final section is the same trail used by the Spinebill Loop in reverse.

Practical Notes

Toilets are at the Eatts St car park at the start; carry all water, as there are nothing on the trail itself. Dogs are not permitted in the reserve. Check gate times with SA Water before visiting — the reserve has seasonal gated hours.

For access, opening hours, facilities and the full rules that apply to every Myponga loop, see the Myponga Reservoir Walks guide.

Practical Information

Tips for Walkers

The Lapwing is the most interesting of the shorter Myponga loops — these notes cover the key practical points.

Allow More Than the Estimated Time

The 1h 10m estimate is for a steady walking pace. Add time if you stop at the lookouts, do any birdwatching, or plan a rest at the Homestead Ruin Lookout. More importantly: check the reserve's current gate times before heading out — gates lock at specific times that change seasonally, and the Lapwing's 1h 10m circuit doesn't leave much buffer if you start late.

The Best Mid-Length Choice at Myponga

Of the four Myponga loops, the Lapwing is the one that earns its length most consistently — it covers the reserve's most interesting terrain (the forest walk section and the homestead ruins) without the full commitment of the Kestrel. If you can only do one loop and have more than 30 minutes, the Lapwing is the right choice.

Birdwatching Through the Forest Section

The forest walk section of the Lapwing — the densest native bush on the circuit — is the best area in the reserve for forest birds. Slow down here and listen. Honeyeaters, grey fantails and thornbills work through the understory; in the mornings the chorus can be genuinely impressive. Lapwings (masked plovers) are a common sighting on the more open reserve margins.

The Homestead Ruins — A Note on History

The Myponga valley was farming land before SA Water developed the reservoir in the 1960s. The homestead ruins visible from the Homestead Ruin Lookout are remnants of the properties that were acquired and cleared when the reserve was established. It's worth pausing here to consider the landscape's layered history before the reservoir was built.
