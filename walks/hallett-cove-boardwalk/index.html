The Walk

Hallett Cove Conservation Park sits 22 km south of Adelaide and contains some of the most remarkable geology accessible on a day walk anywhere in South Australia. The boardwalk trail follows the coastal edge through a reserve that preserves Permian glacial features — striated rock pavements, glacial erratics and the distinctive U-shaped cove — all approximately 280 million years old and among the best-preserved examples on the continent.

The walk is rated Grade 3 Moderate, and that rating fits well for most walkers. The 326 m of total ascent is spread over 11.41 km of out-and-back coastal trail, which means the climbing happens in short, manageable sections rather than sustained single climbs. The surface is varied — boardwalk near the main entry, compacted coastal track further south, with some rocky sections near the geological features.

The Geology

The Permian glaciation at Hallett Cove occurred around 280 million years ago when the area was near the South Pole. An ice sheet moving northward carved the distinctive features now visible in the park: grooved and striated pavements showing the direction of ice movement, large boulders (erratics) carried and deposited by the glacier, and the curved cove itself, shaped by glacial erosion. Information boards along the trail explain what you’re looking at in accessible terms.

This is not just a scenic coastal walk — it’s a geology lesson at continental scale. The striated pavement visible just off the boardwalk is one of the locations included in educational materials about ancient Antarctic glaciation.

New Suspension Bridges

Two suspension bridges were installed in August 2024, replacing previous scramble routes over rocky sections near the boardwalk. The bridges significantly improve accessibility and reduce erosion pressure on the rock pavement beneath. They are single-file crossings — wait at the near end until the group ahead has cleared the bridge before starting your crossing. The views from both bridges are excellent.

Getting There by Train

Hallett Cove Beach train station is the closest to the main boardwalk entry — approximately 5 minutes’ walk. The Noarlunga Centre line connects Adelaide CBD, passing through Adelaide, Edwardstown, Seaton Park and other stations before reaching Hallett Cove Beach. The surrounding stations at Hallett Cove, Christie Downs and Noarlunga Centre are also within walking distance of the trail’s different access points.

This makes Hallett Cove one of the most accessible coastal walks from Adelaide without a car. Check the Adelaide Metro timetable for service frequency — trains typically run every 15–30 minutes during the day.

Trail Access Points

The main access point is the Hallett Cove Beach Reserve carpark at the end of Hallett Cove Beach Road. There is also access from Marino Conservation Park to the north and from Seacliff Park to the south. The Marion Council maintains the coastal walking trail through the reserve.

The Return Leg

Since this is an out-and-back, you return on the same route. The climbing on the return leg is slightly more concentrated than on the outward journey, which catches some walkers off guard. Budget more time and water for the return than you might expect.

Practical Information

Tips for Walkers

A popular walk easily accessible by train — go mid-week or early morning on weekends to avoid the busiest sections near the boardwalk.

Fire Danger Closures

Hallett Cove Conservation Park closes on Catastrophic Fire Danger Rating days. Check the CFS website (cfs.sa.gov.au) or the Parks SA app before visiting. The park may also close on Extreme rating days at management's discretion.

Train Access — Excellent

Hallett Cove Beach train station is about 5 minutes' walk from the main boardwalk entry. The Noarlunga Centre line runs frequently. This makes car-free day trips straightforward — check the Adelaide Metro timetable for service times.

Suspension Bridges — Opened August 2024

Two new suspension bridges were installed in August 2024, significantly improving access on the coastal section. The bridges replace previous scramble routes over rocky outcrops. They are single-file crossings — wait for the party ahead to clear before crossing.

Geological Significance

Hallett Cove Conservation Park preserves one of the finest examples of Permian glaciation in Australia — around 280 million years old. The striated pavements, erratics and U-shaped valley visible on the walk are the remnants of an ancient ice sheet. Information boards along the trail explain the geology.

Water — Carry Everything

There is no drinking water on the trail. A kiosk operates near the main carpark at Hallett Cove Beach on most weekends, but do not rely on it. Carry at least 1.5 litres. The return leg on a warm day is noticeably harder than the outward journey.

Dogs — Partial Access

Dogs on leads are permitted in some sections of the reserve and on the beachfront areas. They are not permitted within the Conservation Park boundaries. Check the signage at each entry point carefully — the boundary is not always obvious to first-time visitors.
