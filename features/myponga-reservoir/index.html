Myponga Reservoir Walks — Complete Guide

Everything you need to know about walking the Myponga Reservoir Reserve, plus every bird-named loop reviewed.

4 walks Myponga fleurieu reservoir loop

Myponga Reservoir is a working drinking-water reservoir on South Australia’s Fleurieu Peninsula, just inland from the township of Myponga and a short drive from Myponga’s well-known weekend market. The surrounding Myponga Reservoir Reserve has been opened by SA Water for low-impact recreation, and its network of gentle walking loops — each named after a local bird — makes it one of the easiest and most relaxed reservoir walks in the region.

This page is the single home for everything that applies to all of the walks here — access, facilities and rules — so the individual trail reviews can stay focused on the walking itself. Read this once, then pick a loop below.

Where It Is & Getting There

The reserve sits just off the main road near Myponga, roughly 70 km south of the Adelaide CBD — about an hour’s drive via Main South Road and Victor Harbor Road. It is an easy add-on to a Fleurieu day trip, the Myponga Market, or a run further south to Second Valley or Cape Jervis.

There are two main access points: the Eatts St car park (eastern loops — Spinebill, Lapwing) and the Hansen St car park (Heron, Kestrel). A car is the practical way to get here.

Opening Hours & Access

As a managed SA Water reserve, Myponga Reservoir Reserve has gated opening hours that change with the season, and entry is free. Gates are locked outside hours, so check the current opening times with SA Water before you go and plan to be back at the car park well before closing — particularly on the longer Kestrel circuit.

Facilities

The reserve is set up for a short, low-key visit rather than a full day out:

  • Sealed car parks at the Eatts St and Hansen St entrances
  • Public toilets at the Eatts St car park and on the western side of the reserve
  • Lookouts over the reservoir, including a timber viewing deck

There are no facilities out on the trails themselves and no drinking water, so fill bottles and use the toilets before you set off.

Rules & What to Know

Because it protects a drinking-water supply, the reserve has firm rules that apply on every walk:

  • No dogs anywhere in the reserve
  • No swimming or water contact in the reservoir
  • Walking only — stay on the formed trails
  • Take all rubbish with you

These rules are consistent across all the loops, which is why you won’t see them repeated on each trail review.

The Trail Network

The walks are a connected family of loops named after local birds, sharing the same trailheads so you can mix and match by time and energy:

  • Spinebill Loop — a flat ~0.7 km stroll to the reservoir lookout. Ideal for a quick stop or small children.
  • Heron Loop — an easy ~1.9 km circuit past the deck and reservoir lookouts.
  • Lapwing Loop — a ~3.9 km bushland loop adding the forest-walk and homestead-ruin lookouts.
  • Kestrel Loop — the full ~6.8 km circuit linking every lookout in the reserve.

Surfaces are mostly formed gravel and bushland trail, with gentle, rolling grades — nothing steep. The reservoir margins and quieter bush sections are good for birdwatching.

Best Time to Visit

March to November is ideal — mild temperatures and green countryside. In the warmer months go early and carry plenty of water, as the open sections have limited shade. Mornings are best for birdlife around the water.

Conditions, gate times and facility availability can change. Treat this guide as a starting point and confirm current details with SA Water before heading out.
